    How the recommendation is made

    Five steps, applied to every site in the same way.

    Step 1Estimate sales two waysWhat local households can spend, and what the passing crowd can buy.
    Step 2Take the lower, calibrateUse the binding limit, scaled so the model matches our own stores' real sales.
    Step 3Subtract cannibalisationSales moved from our nearby stores are not growth; their margin is deducted.
    Step 4Profit and paybackMargin minus rent and running costs; capex divided by yearly gain.
    Step 5Rank and pickDrop sites over the payback bar, score the rest, pick one at a time.

    The formulas

    Wallet sales = households × income × 12% grocery share × modern-trade share × our share vs competitors households = population ÷ 4 modern share = 4% + 4% per ₹1 lakh monthly income our share = pull ÷ (pull + competitors), pull = 0.7 + 0.06 × access Traffic sales = footfall × conversion × basket × 360 days Sales = min(wallet, traffic) × calibration factor Store EBITDA = sales × (gross margin − 4%) − rent − ₹1.6 cr staff & utilities Lost margin = sales × cannibalisation share × gross margin Net gain = store EBITDA − lost margin Payback = capex ÷ net gain

    Why it is built this way

    • Two ceilings, not one. A busy commuter street with few residents and a rich suburb with little passing trade both fail, for different reasons. Taking the lower of the two stops either from looking better than it is.
    • Calibrated on our own stores. The raw model is scaled so it reproduces what our existing stores really sell, which anchors the forecast in the chain's own performance.
    • Chain view, not store view. A new store that mostly moves customers from our existing store adds little. Only the net gain to the chain counts.
    • Hard bar, then judgement. The payback bar removes sites that cannot earn back their capex; the weights then reflect management's priorities among the survivors.
    • Portfolio, not list. Picks are made one at a time and each pick is treated as an existing store when rating the next, so two new stores don't share the same shoppers.

    Key limitation

    Catchments are straight-line circles. Mumbai shoppers follow rail lines, creeks and flyovers, so true catchments are long and thin along the suburban railway. Cannibalisation and competition should be confirmed with travel-time catchments and loyalty-card data on where customers actually live before signing a lease.

    Also: the model is calibrated on only four stores, so treat sales forecasts as ±15%; economics are year-1 steady state with no ramp-up, inflation, tax or discounting.

    Business value

    • Every site is judged by the same rules, so the capex decision is consistent and easy to defend.
    • It shows trade-offs, not just a winner: why the busiest or richest site was not chosen.
    • The rent ceiling for each site gives the property team a walk-away number for negotiation.
    • The stress test shows which picks are safe and which need validation first.
    • New sites can be screened in minutes from a spreadsheet, before paying for surveys.
